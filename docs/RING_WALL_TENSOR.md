# Loaded-ring wall and gap control

## Protocol before acquisition

Run the published Lumerical geometry at 16 and 20 cells/µm using identical fixed material indices, nominal 0.65 µm PML, 1.8 µm nominal port aperture, 150 µm/c straight reference and 3,200 µm/c device. Its 100 nm edge gap spans 1.6 and 2 cells respectively. Compare each to its existing scalar exact-area run at the same mesh. Use the distinct Tidy3D geometry and cladding in a separate control at 16 cells/µm and 4,800 µm/c. Keep the physical gap, radius, material indices and wavelength axis fixed.

Use exact disk and strip area fractions at each staggered E-row location. Model each partially filled sidewall by tangential arithmetic and normal harmonic permittivity; rotate the inverse tensor into grid coordinates using the local annulus radius. Straight bus normals point along y. Keep the prior Ez vertical-column mixture. Cells crossing both a horizontal and a vertical face use sequential mixing. The radial normal approximates curvature inside a cell. This is a controlled constitutive approximation. The Maxwell update uses a lossless specialization of the FDTDX nine-entry electric update. [Meep subpixel smoothing](https://meep.readthedocs.io/en/master/Subpixel_Smoothing/) · [Farjadpour et al.](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.31.002972).

Evaluate all four complex outputs of one bus excitation and the associated powers, across identical wavelengths without freely shifting peaks. Apply the existing straight-through, passivity, incident-mode and 80%-duration gates. Retain the existing 0.05 mean absolute power error and peak-within-one-published-linewidth gates. Report 1550 nm errors separately. Compare strong-port phases only after moving to common planes and accounting for port gauge. Report weak-port powers, early-window changes and absolute complex amplitudes even when their phases are undefined or unreliable. A power match alone cannot establish complex S-parameter parity. The pinned Tidy3D example gives through/drop intensity curves; phase and weak-port values require independent reference data. Archive measured timings with native arrays. The four-port column here measures one launch. Four independent launches are needed for a full matrix.

## Independent tensor-update check

A one-dimensional periodic wave exercises the native nine-entry FDTDX update independently of the ring launches. A linearly polarized initial field excites both principal polarizations of a uniform tensor rotated by 36°. The analytical discrete Yee recurrence predicts the two component histories. The measured orthogonal field reached 0.94433 for an initial amplitude of 1. Over 256 steps the maximum component error was 5.19 × 10⁻⁷ against the predeclared 2 × 10⁻⁵ limit. This CPU control checks rotation, tensor component order, interpolation in this propagation direction and the time update. The device studies exercise the additional curved interfaces and PML. ## Solver and timing provenance

An initial dense nine-entry device attempt failed with a CUDA kernel launch error after the first 2,240 recorded samples. Its incomplete device output did not enter the comparison. We preserved its successful straight reference locally. Other instrumented acquisitions on the same WSL device also reported launch errors. The production workers disable optional CUPTI tracing and retain synchronized GPU step time. We cannot attribute the failed ring launch to a specific kernel or to the tracer from this observation alone.

The production worker uses the lossless identity A = I and B = c ε⁻¹ in FDTDX’s electric update. Only the xy/yx off-diagonal entries can be nonzero for the extruded vertical walls. The worker reuses FDTDX’s curl, six-face PML recurrences, staggered interpolation, source injection, magnetic update and post-boundary update. The reference contains only aligned straight walls and exactly zero off-diagonal entries. Complex port bases come from the identical straight sections in reference and device. At the source and all four monitor planes the off-diagonal entries are checked to be zero.

Besides the plane-wave test, we advanced random 3D E and H fields through a spatially varying xy tensor with a plane source and six PML faces, using the published dense update and the specialized update separately. At step 13 the maximum absolute differences were 9.69 × 10⁻⁸ in E, 1.19 × 10⁻⁷ in H and 8.94 × 10⁻⁸ in both PML memory fields. Those amplitudes are relative to fields of order 0.5. The predeclared absolute pass limit was 2 × 10⁻⁵. Both methods also pass the 256-step analytic eigenwave check. [Native field controls](verification/v014-ring-wall-wave.json).

Acquisitions record source hashes, GPU model, grid shape, physical cell spacing and the six actual PML face counts after rounding to cells. The receipt’s curved_inverse_xy_nonzero_cells counts staggered nonzero xy/yx coefficients; a single logical cell can contain both. The step scope synchronizes native E/H plus projections. Setup, compilation, synchronized evolution and CUDA event elapsed time have separate fields. The event interval includes launch and idle time. Per-kernel active time is unavailable. The total includes cells inside the PML. The non-PML count explicitly excludes the combined boundary volume. The timing labels distinguish fresh acquisitions from reused receipts.

## Measured Lumerical 16-cell control

The tensor model brought the spacing of the central local drop peaks to 25.5 nm (scalar 26.0 nm; filed 25.4549 nm). The source-mode index at 1550 nm fell from 1.97062 to 1.91810. At fixed, jointly qualified vendor wavelengths the broadband through/drop absolute power errors fell from 0.16457/0.13545 to 0.13095/0.08478. The reflection/add-side errors increased from 0.00319/0.00317 to 0.00900/0.00899. These use 198 common vendor-band samples with no resonance registration.

At 1550 nm the drop changed from 3.1605% to 0.29248%, compared with filed 67.44231%. The through power was 99.41467% against filed 9.39153%. Reflection was 0.00007287% against filed 0.076158%, and add-side power was 0.0010960% against 0.082454%. A nearly equal central peak spacing coexists with a 12.09 nm difference between the closest local drop peak and the filed 1550.092 nm peak. The filed peak’s half-maximum width is 1.421 nm. The nearest-peak assignment alone cannot establish common angular mode order.

The original 3,200 µm/c recording passes 236 of 241 wavelength checks. The five exclusions at 1490.0, 1490.5, 1511.5, 1512.0 and 1514.0 nm all arose from the finite observation window; the 80%-to-final power changes were 9.55, 5.36, 6.07, 7.19 and 6.13 percentage points. Each of these wavelengths passed the incident, straight, total collected power and launch-ratio checks. The all-bin gate failed. The solver wrote fields and complex projections for every wavelength. ## Measured Lumerical 20-cell control

All 241 native wavelengths qualified. At 1550 nm the scalar exact-area model gave 1.1225% drop; the rotated tensor gives 50.1835%, against filed 67.4423%. Through power is 19.3189% against 9.3915%. Through/drop broadband MAEs fell from 0.17233/0.13646 to 0.02063/0.01823. The nearest drop peak is 0.408 nm from the filed central peak, within its 1.421 nm linewidth. At this mesh, the scalar and tensor launch indices are identical at stored precision. Their reference E and H profiles differ by less than 7.2 × 10⁻⁸ relative to each array’s peak. The change at this mesh therefore isolates the curved-device treatment far more closely than at 16 cells/µm.

The weak ports behave differently. At 1550 nm reflected power is 7.5741% against filed 0.07616%, and add-side power is 7.5477% against 0.08245%. The ring projects a reflected amplitude of 0.27521; the straight-reference background amplitude is 0.0000657. The 80%-to-full change of the complex reflection is 0.00242, 0.88% of this observed amplitude. Neither the straight subtraction nor this finite-window variation accounts for the excess. The r16→r20 maximum changes over their jointly qualified vendor band were 97.7 percentage points through, 79.0 drop, 22.2 reflection and 22.1 add-side. Cross-mesh comparisons also retain the actual rounded PML and aperture widths. [20-cell native measurement](verification/v014-ring3d-lumerical-r20-t3200-wall-tensor.json) · [Adjacent mesh comparison](verification/v014-ring-wall-meshes.json).

A direct Fourier transform at 0.05 nm intervals around the central peak reproduces every original 0.5 nm complex sample. The local reflected/add powers both peak at 1550.45 nm (11.3628% / 11.3213%). Over this band they differ by at most 0.0415 percentage points, and their relative phase changes by less than 0.009 rad where both exceed 1% power. Each return has one resolved peak of approximately 1.255 nm FWHM. The early-window peak remains at the same sampled wavelength, with a width of 1.287 nm. The full observation’s approximate Fourier resolution is 0.764 nm. The drop has one resolved peak near 1550.40 nm, approximately 2.216 nm wide. The filed reflected peak within the band is also near this wavelength (1550.493 nm), yet reaches only 0.19065%; simple resonance registration does not explain the return-power difference.

These observations support a common resonant return channel, while its physical origin requires further evidence. Three Hz point histories and real instantaneous midplane E snapshots do not separate clockwise and counterclockwise angular modes. We cannot assign a standing-mode doublet from a single unresolved line, infer S₁₁ = S₄₁ from reciprocity, or establish agreement from the 20-cell strong-port fit alone. [Measured return line shapes and filed samples](verification/v014-ring20-return-band.json) · [Matched-plane phase analysis](verification/v014-ring-phase-wall.json).

The independent temporal loss check also withholds the central 20-cell peak. Its demodulated probe/window decay estimates span roughly 847–1090 in Q (25.7% relative spread). The narrow-band drop FWHM describes the recorded line shape. The available evidence does not qualify intrinsic/external loss separation there. [Temporal and spectral fits](verification/v014-ring3d-lumerical-r20-t3200-wall-tensor.json).

## Longer Lumerical 16-cell observation

A fresh 6,000 µm/c device run reused only the verified straight reference. Its first 3,200 µm/c of port projections and three ring Hz histories reproduced the shorter run exactly. At 4,800 µm/c all 241 bins qualified, with a maximum 80%-to-full change of 1.674 percentage points. At 6,000 µm/c all 241 qualified, with a maximum change of 1.028 points. This gain came from the longer observation with identical materials, gap, mesh and ports.

At 1550 nm the longer run gives 0.28774% drop and 99.52656% through. The 67.44231% filed drop remains far outside the local result. Reflected power moves from 0.00007287% to 0.00000610%, illustrating why an absolute power gate is insufficient for a phase claim about such a small amplitude. The 167,955-step device stage took 655.521 s synchronized / 655.923 s CUDA elapsed. The straight stage’s original acquisition is referenced separately and incurred zero new field steps in this invocation. ## Held-out Tidy3D 16-cell control

The separate 5 µm, oxide-clad ring passed all 241 native wavelength checks. At 1550 nm the 16-cell result gives 99.275% through and 0.692% drop. The digitized figure gives 98.940% and 2.159%. Using the two plot columns straddling 1550 nm and the color stroke plus half a vertical pixel, through power reads between 96.85% and 101.07%, and drop between 1.88% and 2.48%. These intervals describe plot readout only; the plotted stroke can extend above 100%. Local through power falls within that stroke, while drop remains below it. The older scalar model gave 28.839% through and 69.390% drop at this same wavelength.

Across 201 jointly qualified band samples, through and drop MAEs are 0.15994 and 0.12535. The physical simulation cost was 818.732 s synchronized / 819.005 s CUDA elapsed for 134,365 device steps on 3,304,000 cells. The public example shows through and drop intensity; it does not supply independent complex or reflected/add arrays for a vendor-phase check. ## Held-out Tidy3D 20-cell control

The 20-cell run qualified 240/241 native samples. The remaining sample is at 1499 nm, beyond the published comparison band. Its through-power change from 80% to full duration was 5.129 percentage points, just above the predeclared 5-point limit. All 201 samples in the 1500–1600 nm vendor band pass. The native all-bin result remains partial.

At 1550 nm, through power changed from 99.275% at 16 cells/µm to 15.914% at 20. Drop changed from 0.692% to 83.682%, compared with digitized 98.940% through and 2.159% drop. The 20-cell central local drop peak lies at 1550 nm while the published one is near 1547 nm. Across the vendor band, nearest peak offsets improve to +2.58 to +4.29 nm but still exceed the published 0.85–1.40 nm linewidths. Broadband MAE is 0.12923 through and 0.10995 drop. Central FSR remains 17.50 nm on both meshes against 17.74 nm published.

The largest through and drop changes across the two meshes reach 99.28 and 93.21 percentage points. At common workbench planes (|x| = 5.76 µm), the corresponding strong-power RMS phase changes are 1.217 and 2.087 rad. At the example’s |x| = 5.0 µm planes the independent port-phase analysis gives 1.074 rad through and 1.875 rad drop, using its own amplitude and timing gates. A useful single-wavelength match on the coarser geometry therefore does not establish mesh convergence. The 20-cell device evolved 167,952 steps on 6,464,640 cells, using 1,966.941 s synchronized / 1,967.233 s CUDA elapsed. The hardware budget admitted this run with a 40% scaling margin and an additional 2048 MiB reserve.
