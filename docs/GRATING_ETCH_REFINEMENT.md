# Finite-width grating mesh refinement

The Lumerical benchmark contains 38 curved teeth in a 220 nm silicon layer with a 100 nm etch. A tilted step-index fiber launches the input, and the observable is power captured in the output strip's backward TE₀ mode. The Tidy3D benchmark uses a different device with straight teeth, a 160 nm etch, a 50 µm taper and an air-side Gaussian launch. Each workbench spectrum is compared with its own published design. [Ansys example and S-parameter data](https://optics.ansys.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042305334-Grating-coupler) · [Tidy3D example](https://www.flexcompute.com/tidy3d/examples/notebooks/GratingCoupler/)

## Numerical controls

The workbench integrates the Lumerical grating's curved interfaces on each electric-component control volume. At 12, 14, 16 and 20 cells/µm, the 100 nm etch occupies 1.2, 1.4, 1.6 and 2 grid intervals, respectively. Partial-cell integration preserves the modeled etch depth across these meshes. It cannot remove propagation and scattering error from the finite Yee lattice.

Both devices and their launches are symmetric in y. For the chosen Ey polarization the symmetry plane has zero tangential electric field. A native PEC plane can replace half the y domain. A single negative-side sample on an even full detector may map to the positive-side cell just beyond that detector. The worker records that extra sample, restores the missing neighbor in FDTDX's co-location average at y=0, applies odd parity to Ex, Ez and Hy, and even parity to Ey, Hx and Hz. It reconstructs the original full source, observation planes and output port eigenmode before evaluating flux, complex amplitudes and weak returns. Energy decay uses the native allocated volume. [FDTDX PEC boundary](https://fdtdx.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/fdtdx.PerfectElectricConductor.html) · [Detector reconstruction](../scripts/grating_mirror.py)

At every wavelength, the 12-cell Lumerical mirror control matched the full-domain launch amplitudes and component delays bit-for-bit. The largest power error was 0.0000161 percentage points, the largest guided phase change was 0.000000515 radians, and the largest weak fiber-return amplitude change was 0.000000211. The reference and device stored 3,061,760 native cells each. Synchronized stepping took 21.57 s for 2,646 reference steps and 56.16 s for 7,560 device steps. ## GPU admission and timing

The finer full-grid Lumerical calculation uses the documented JAX on-demand allocator. A host-resident shape plan compiles the actual CUDA program. A native one-step experiment at an already simulated mesh measures device allocation and samples GPU memory use throughout setup. The admission rule takes the larger of live-buffer scaling and cell-count scaling, then adds 20% and 1,024 MiB. Both incident and device programs must fit. The recorded 12-cell extrapolation required 13,407 MiB at 16 cells/µm. The nearer 14-cell measurement required 9,092 MiB because the observed allocation at 14 stayed close to the allocation at 12. The worker checks currently available framebuffer memory under an exclusive GPU lock before each acquisition. [Measured plans and native allocations](verification/v014-grating-memory-lumerical-r16.json) · [JAX memory allocation](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/gpu_memory_allocation.html)

Performance metadata identifies the CUDA device, allocated grid including PML, step count, physical timestep, compilation time, synchronized stepping and stage duration. Finer production runs also use a CUDA event clock. Its elapsed interval includes device launch gaps. Kernel-active time remains a separate measurement. A cache hit reports the earlier acquisition and records the current request's cache status and lock wait. Two experimental in-process CUPTI runs failed before recording usable spectra. ## Interpreting the remaining difference

The public Lumerical file gives silicon and oxide permittivity at all five monitored wavelengths. Both local fixed indices use the filed 1550 nm values. A continuous TE₀ slab calculation isolates the size of the material-dispersion contribution away from band center. It solves for oxide-clad 220 nm ridge and 120 nm etched-film modes, takes the filed fill-weighted change in propagation constant, and applies it to a common 25 µm length. Relative to frozen indices, the phase change is +0.396 rad at 1500 nm, zero at 1550 nm, and −0.473 rad at 1600 nm. These are auxiliary phase estimates for uniform sections. Edge scattering, curved propagation, taper transport and fiber coupling enter the full-device measurements. The center-wavelength power mismatch therefore requires checking more than the off-center index variation. [Filed material samples](verification/v012-grating-commercial-references.json) · [Reproducible calculation](../scripts/summarize_grating_refinement.py)

The filed Lumerical simulation lattice has 43.09 nm x spacing at the beam, 42.92 nm y spacing at the symmetry plane, and 43.10 nm z spacing through the etch. Its 100 nm etch spans 2.32 equivalent intervals. The 50 nm local candidate represents the same depth across 2 intervals. The matched Tidy3D notebook, reconstructed locally with Tidy3D 2.11.2, uses 29.72 nm x and 29.67 nm y spacing there. Its z grid samples the 160 nm etch in six 26.67 nm intervals. The local 62.5 nm candidate spans 2.56 intervals through that etch. Matching a vendor's nominal mesh setting alone would miss these differences in local cell sizes. [Filed and reconstructed mesh samples](verification/v014-grating-vendor-mesh.json)

The memory-profile probes count exactly one native step per observed baseline and zero native steps for each candidate shape plan. The shape-plan compilation timers are recorded in those profiles. The one-step probes in this campaign did not record separate synchronized stepping or CUDA event durations. Their run count is retained with unavailable step time rather than assigning compilation or process lifetime to electromagnetic computation.
