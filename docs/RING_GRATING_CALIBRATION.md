# Ring spectra and grating radiation

These local CPU studies answer different optical questions. The ring study launches one guided mode and records the through and drop spectra. The grating study measures the fraction of incoming guided power radiated upward and separately measures how much of a specified incoming Gaussian beam couples into a guide.

## Ring add/drop filter

The Meep model propagates a planar magnetic Hz field. It uses the published lateral ring and bus layout, and evaluates the symmetric vertical slab's fundamental TE index at 1.55 µm from the declared core thickness. The in-plane solver holds that index fixed over the sweep. This removes vertical radiation and vertical index dispersion. Those omissions matter for both free spectral range (FSR) and linewidth. An independent finite-height MPB strip calculation reports the straight-guide group-delay estimate λ²/(2πR n_g). This estimate also omits bend and coupling phase. Its value is kept separate from the measured ring spectrum.

A pulse excites the lower left bus. A device simulation and a matched straight two-bus reference record synchronized Ey and Hz profiles on all four physical sections. The discrete Fourier transform and electric/magnetic overlap with the outgoing and incoming slab profiles yield directional amplitudes. The incident amplitude comes from the straight reference. The report includes the four complex outgoing coefficients for that launch, the straight-through control, missing port power, an 80%-duration comparison, and isolated mesh, PML, clearance and aperture controls. All port powers use the same physical normalization. Loaded linewidth Q is supplied only when the half-height span covers four spectral samples and at least twice the post-source finite-window frequency interval. Closer peaks keep their wavelength and power while Q is withheld. Adjacent peaks nearest the band center define the local FSR. The arrays retain the reference and device amplitudes, early-cut result and measured time trace on the ring.

**Bare ring in three dimensions.** A separate full-vector finite-height Maxwell calculation keeps the radial and vertical dimensions of the ring. Cylindrical symmetry represents angular variation as exp(i m φ) and excludes both bus guides. A finite-height straight-strip mode selects the initial angular order. The search measures neighboring orders across the target wavelength. An Er pulse excites the ring and two disjoint post-source Harminv windows independently extract the pole. The raw archive contains both Er and Ez, the observation times, pole error, polarization checks, mesh comparison, and boundary sensitivity. The comparison pairs a measured *bare* ring FSR with a published *bus-loaded* FSR. Neither the bus-loaded linewidth nor through/drop power follows from an isolated pole. [Meep cylindrical-coordinate formulation](https://meep.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Python_Tutorials/Cylindrical_Coordinates/)

**Ansys Lumerical.** The 3.1 µm ring has 0.40 µm wide, 0.18 µm high guides and a 0.10 µm gap. The public 3D FDTD text attachment tabulates 500 samples per scattering entry. For the port-1 drive, the magnitude squared of S21 gives drop power. The principal peaks in that attachment occur near 1.52464, 1.55009 and 1.57724 µm. The shortest-wavelength resonance also has a weaker split partner. These measured values and the raw attachment SHA are the external reference. The 25.6 nm value in the design tutorial is a target. The physical 3D guide has an oxide substrate and upper air, with a dispersive commercial silicon model. The supplementary finite-height mode estimate uses those cladding indices and a constant silicon index of 3.48. [Ansys device and exported matrix](https://optics.ansys.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042800213-Ring-resonator-getting-started-Final-parameter-extraction) · [Published numeric file](https://optics.ansys.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/50334805245587)

**Tidy3D.** The 5 µm ring has 0.43 µm wide, 0.22 µm high guides, a 0.10 µm gap and oxide above and below. Local and published guide dimensions agree. The vendor used 3D dispersive materials, guided-mode monitors and a 30 ps recording. The external wavelengths are digitized from the full published spectrum with about 0.25 nm readout uncertainty per peak. ## Grating radiation and fiber-like excitation

The Meep model resolves the finite silicon thickness, grooves, top oxide, BOX and silicon substrate in the x–z cross section. It is invariant across the lateral aperture. A straight reference measures the power of a TE0 guided launch. Four flux planes enclose the grating. The upper and lower planes report radiated fractions and the end planes collect all power leaving the sides. Their sum measures the power-balance error. Each wavelength is marked separately if that sum differs from unity by 0.04 or more. The separate Gaussian curve flags bins with less than 1% of peak reference power or a coupled fraction above 1.02. Lateral-plane power can include nonguided radiation. A separate homogeneous-air reference measures the incoming downward Gaussian flux. The grating's negative-x fundamental modal power divided by that reference gives beam-to-guide efficiency. Upper-half-space flux and Gaussian coupling are different observables.

**Tidy3D.** Its 2D uniform grating uses 20 periods of 0.620 µm at 80% fill, 0.260 µm silicon with a 0.160 µm etch, 2.0 µm BOX and a top oxide ending at height 0.680 µm. The 14.5° beam has 5.4 µm waist radius centered at x = 3.6 µm. Its published optimized 2D coupling is 56.23% at 1.55 µm. The local model preserves these physical parameters and the published nondispersive indices 3.47 and 1.44. The finite source, calibration plane, lateral clearance and grid differ. A supplemental run uses the notebook’s shorter computational box, clips the Gaussian to the non-PML part of that box while retaining its optical axis, and places the output monitor at the notebook’s x coordinate. Its assumed uniform-grid absorber thickness is recorded separately. The vendor's full 3D grating includes a finite width and a 50 µm access taper, and reports about −4 dB. The separate slab-mode first-order pitch estimate gives 607.541 nm versus the tutorial’s rounded 608 nm seed. [Published geometry, normalization and 2D result](https://www.flexcompute.com/tidy3d/examples/notebooks/GratingCoupler/)

**Ansys Lumerical.** Its separate 1.310 µm grating is 50 µm long, has 0.500 µm pitch, 50% fill, a 0.030 µm etch and a 0.200 µm silicon film. The public FSP confirms these dimensions and a nominal silicon index of 3.4993. The measured published maximum is 55.7% at 13.9°. The reconstruction uses the published 13 µm source-position estimate and a 17.3 µm Gaussian waist within the article's quoted range. The article-based reconstruction uses oxide index 1.447. The separate project-source control uses the FSP’s constant material indices of 3.4993 and 1.44. The filed geometry also includes a final 0.25 µm etched interval between the last tooth and the output guide. Its 0.70 µm BOX lies above the silicon substrate, and the upper oxide ends 0.90 µm above the grating base. The base lies at global y = −1.3 µm. The source center (14.8, 2.0) µm therefore lies 3.3 µm above that base. The FSP records width parameter w0 = 17.288 µm, lensZ0 = −3.2 µm and an incidence angle of 13.9°. The local Gaussian places its waist 3.2 µm downstream along the optical axis. This maps the reported focal displacement into a 2D Gaussian approximation to the vendor’s thin-lens profile. Both models use a finite 67.5 µm local injection line and an independently measured empty-air reference. The analytical slab effective-index weighting gives 2.86341 versus the article’s phase-matching value of approximately 2.86. [Ansys broadband grating](https://optics.ansys.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042304774-Broadband-grating-coupler-2D) · [Published project](https://optics.ansys.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/360057295433)

## Numerical evidence

The compact Tidy3D-sized grating measured **58.2341%** beam-to-guide coupling at 1550 nm, **+2.0041 percentage points** relative to 56.23%. The finely meshed, extended-box control measured **49.9851%** at the same wavelength, **−6.2449 points**. Its outgoing guide excitation sent **69.0448%** upward and **21.2992%** downward; the four enclosing fluxes summed to **100.4050%**. Extending the observation interval from 100 to 160 µm/c changed beam-to-guide coupling from 49.9851% to 49.9868%. The two domain results show that a source aperture and its boundary placement can matter considerably more than that time extension. A fresh Python replay and an executed Jupyter notebook reproduced the compact result, with a maximum difference of 1.71 × 10⁻¹¹ in the raw complex arrays. ## Reproduce locally

Run the programs with the managed Meep/MPB interpreter from the workbench source checkout and choose fresh output directories:

```bash
"$MEEP_PYTHON" scripts/calibrate_ring.py --output ring-results
"$MEEP_PYTHON" scripts/calibrate_grating.py --output grating-results
"$MEEP_PYTHON" scripts/calibrate_ring.py --isolated-only --output bare-ring-results
"$MEEP_PYTHON" scripts/calibrate_grating.py --beam-only tidy3d --vendor-box --output compact-grating-results
"$MEEP_PYTHON" scripts/calibrate_grating.py --beam-only lumerical --project-source --output project-grating-results
```

Each run writes its inputs, numerical comparisons and sensitivities to `report.json` and the corresponding measured arrays to `arrays.npz`. The full campaigns repeat independent excitations on several meshes, with a substantial local CPU runtime. FDTDX CPU/NVIDIA routes for these particular ring and grating measurements remain open.
